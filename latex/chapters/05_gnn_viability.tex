\section{\texorpdfstring{\latestrevision{Shared GNN Viability Check}}{Shared GNN Viability Check}}
\label{subsec:gnn-viability}

{\color{red}
Before screening individual graph-design choices, one short check verifies that
a shared, size-independent graph actor can reach the performance of the tuned
MLP baseline. Otherwise, the screens that follow would optimize an actor family
that is already too weak on the source grid. This exploratory comparison is a
viability check, not one of the controlled Screens B--F.

\paragraph{Question.}
Can one shared GNN encoder serve all agents without sacrificing
\texttt{bus14} survival?

\paragraph{Evidence.}
The optimized MLP baseline reaches $93.4\%$ final and $98.6\%$ peak mean
survival. The strongest shared graph model, \texttt{Optimized Light GINE},
reaches $95.7\%$ final and $97.4\%$ peak survival under the preliminary GINE
protocol. Table~\ref{tab:gnn-viability-compact} summarizes the comparison. The
training curves, remaining exploratory variants, and their configurations are
retained in Appendix~\ref{app:gine-viability-details}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The shared GNN is competitive with the optimized MLP on \texttt{bus14}, so
parameter sharing is not an obvious performance bottleneck. The exploratory
runs also support retaining the centralized MLP critic. Because several
choices changed together in this preliminary campaign, it is not used to rank
individual graph components; the controlled screens begin in
Section~\ref{sec:screen-eepth-width}.
}
