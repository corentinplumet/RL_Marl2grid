\chapter{Thesis Plan}

\section{Plan Inspiration}

This plan follows the structure of the reference thesis in
\texttt{mAI2024deMolB.pdf}: start from the power-grid motivation and research
questions, introduce the scientific background, describe the environment before
the algorithms, then separate methods from results and close by answering the
research questions. The adapted structure below is tuned to this repository:
Grid2Op topology control, decentralized MAPPO, graph encoders, rerun ablations,
and sparse intervention mechanisms.

\section{Working Title}

\textbf{Decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Sparse Power Grid
Topology Control}

\section{Central Thesis Question}

How can decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning agents learn reliable
power-grid topology control policies that preserve survival performance while
scaling to combinatorial action spaces and avoiding unnecessary interventions?

\section{Research Questions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item How should the Grid2Op topology-control problem be formulated as a
  decentralized multi-agent decision problem?
  \item Which architectural choices improve survival and generalization:
  flat MLP policies, graph neural network encoders, shared actor weights,
  critic design, or optimized critic updates?
  \item How do initialization and training choices, especially the initial
  do-nothing bias and reward normalization, affect exploration and learned
  behavior?
  \item Can sparse-control mechanisms, such as rho-based overrides,
  intervention gates, fixed penalties, or adaptive intervention budgets,
  reduce unnecessary topology actions without sacrificing episodic survival?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Expected Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A clear decentralized formulation of topology optimization in Grid2Op.
  \item A controlled comparison of MAPPO baselines, GNN encoders, critic
  variants, and initialization choices.
  \item An ablation study of sparse intervention mechanisms for more
  operator-like control.
  \item A reproducible experiment and analysis pipeline based on W\&B histories,
  cached metrics, notebooks, and final evaluation scripts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Chapter Outline}

\subsection*{Chapter 1: Introduction}

\textbf{Goal.} Motivate topology control as a low-cost but difficult operational
tool for power-grid reliability.

\textbf{Planned content.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Energy-transition context: higher demand, renewable uncertainty, and
  stressed transmission networks.
  \item Topology control as an alternative or complement to redispatching and
  grid expansion.
  \item Why the problem is hard: sequential control, physical constraints,
  rare failures, and combinatorial topology actions.
  \item Why decentralized MARL is a natural fit: local substations, regional
  decision making, reduced per-agent action spaces, and scalability.
  \item Research questions, contributions, and thesis outline.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Chapter 2: Background and Related Work}

\textbf{Goal.} Give the reader enough power-systems and RL background to
understand the design choices.

\textbf{Planned content.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Power-grid operation: substations, busbars, line loading, overloads,
  contingencies, and remedial actions.
  \item Grid2Op and the Learning to Run a Power Network setting.
  \item Reinforcement learning basics: MDPs, policy gradients, PPO, and MAPPO.
  \item Multi-agent reinforcement learning: decentralized execution,
  centralized training, credit assignment, and non-stationarity.
  \item Graph neural networks for grid-structured observations.
  \item Safe, sparse, and constrained RL ideas relevant to intervention budgets.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Chapter 3: Environment and Problem Formulation}

\textbf{Goal.} Define exactly what the agents observe, choose, optimize, and are
evaluated on.

\textbf{Planned content.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Grid2Op environment used in the repository and the selected chronics.
  \item Multi-agent decomposition: agent-region or agent-substation assignment.
  \item Observation spaces: local grid state, line loadings, topology state, and
  optional graph observations.
  \item Action spaces: local topology actions, action 0 as do nothing, legal
  action constraints, and action-space reduction.
  \item Reward and evaluation metrics: episodic survival, illegal action rate,
  action-0 fraction, non-idle fraction, and train/test chronic split.
  \item Reproducibility setup: seeds, W\&B logging, cached histories, and
  deterministic evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Chapter 4: Methods}

\textbf{Goal.} Present the implemented learning systems and the ablations.

\textbf{Planned content.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item MAPPO baseline: actor, critic, rollout collection, PPO objective, and
  centralized training assumptions.
  \item Baseline MLP agents and the MLP baseline family of reruns.
  \item Graph-encoder agents: bus graph construction, GINE/GAT/GCN variants,
  node and edge pre-encoders, graph readout, and flat-feature concatenation.
  \item Action heads: the action-index MLP head and the candidate-action head
  that scores each action from the explicit-line graph nodes it touches.
  \item Critic variants: GNN critic versus MLP critic, legacy versus optimized
  critic updates.
  \item Initialization and exploration choices: entropy coefficient, entropy
  decay, and initial do-nothing probability.
  \item Sparse-control mechanisms: evaluation-time rho heuristic, intervention
  gate, fixed sparse-action penalties, and adaptive intervention budgets.
  \item Experimental protocol for ablations: controlled seeds, fixed evaluation
  cadence, and fair baseline comparisons.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Chapter 5: Experiments and Results}

\textbf{Goal.} Show which design choices matter and what behaviors the agents
learn.

\textbf{Planned content.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Main baseline performance: training stability and test survival.
  \item GNN architecture ablations: concat-flat, actor sharing, GINE versus GAT
  or weighted GCN, light versus full encoders, and critic design.
  \item Action-head ablation: candidate-action scoring versus the action-index
  head on the explicit-line graph, on \texttt{bus14} first and then on the
  reduced WCCI action space.
  \item MLP baseline rerun ablations: optimized critic updates, smaller MLP, reward
  normalization, and do-nothing initialization bias.
  \item Sparse-control ablations: fixed penalty, global-safe weighting,
  local-safe adaptive budget, rho-threshold sensitivity, and budget target
  sensitivity.
  \item Behavioral analysis: how often agents act, whether interventions are
  local, and whether high survival comes with excessive topology churn.
  \item Generalization: seen versus unseen chronics and robustness across seeds.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Figures and tables to prepare.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Table of run families and what each ablation changes.
  \item Survival curves for paper baseline, GNN, MLP baseline, and sparse-control comparisons.
  \item Action-frequency plots: action 0 fraction, any non-idle action, and
  multi-agent simultaneous interventions.
  \item Summary table with final survival, peak survival, and action sparsity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Chapter 6: Discussion}

\textbf{Goal.} Interpret the results rather than just restating them.

\textbf{Planned content.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Answer each research question directly.
  \item Explain which components appear essential, useful, or unnecessary.
  \item Discuss the tradeoff between survival and sparse interventions.
  \item Relate learned behavior to operator-like control: act rarely, act
  locally, and act when the grid is unsafe.
  \item Discuss limits: simulator scope, incomplete operational constraints,
  seed variance, training cost, and dependency on chosen chronics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Chapter 7: Conclusion and Future Work}

\textbf{Goal.} Close with the main thesis claim and next research directions.

\textbf{Planned content.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Short summary of the problem, approach, and core findings.
  \item Final conclusion about decentralized MARL for topology control.
  \item Future work: richer graph representations, larger grids, outage
  contingencies, constrained RL tuning, better coordination mechanisms, and
  operational validation.
\end{itemize}
